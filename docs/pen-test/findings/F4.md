# F4: the cost of a Family's growing event history

> **Personal use only.** Wizzaroo is intended for your own personal use only. It is not intended for use with or by a school, teacher, parent or student. Only use it for yourself.

The findings note for scope issue #218 (F4) of [the first pen-test round](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md), which answers the cost half of [H10](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md#h10-a-device-can-write-any-event-its-family-could-have-earned--medium). It names a way to stall the server, so it is kept private on the same terms as the PRD.

- **Trust boundary:** what a Family Device's uploads cost the server, and who else pays for them. A device's history is the Family's to write ([ADR 0004](../../architectural-decision-record/0004-offline-play-records-events-the-server-decides.md)). How long the server spends on it, and whose requests wait meanwhile, is the boundary probed here.
- **Code:**
  - `loadFamilyRewards` in `src/server/children/family-rewards.ts` reads every rewarded event the Family has ever uploaded and replays them all with `applyPlayRecords` in `src/curriculum/rewards.ts`;
  - `POST /api/play/events` runs that replay once per upload, through `refreshRoomShowcases` (`src/server/children/room-showcases.ts`);
  - `GET /api/play/family` runs it once per opening, both in `src/server/family-devices/`;
  - `addPracticeDay` and `buildSkillMastery` in `src/curriculum/rewards.ts` and `mastery.ts` do the per-event work.
- **Probes:** the `pen test F4: the cost of a Family's growing event history` block in `src/integration.test.ts`, in the gated run against a real Postgres. It loads [the fixtures](../fixtures.md) into a database of its own and grows two Families' histories:
  - **Family A1, by ordinary play:** 100 questions a day, one Skill practised for ten days and then the next, every third answer wrong. The first 100 events go up through `familyDeviceA1`'s own upload, and the rest are inserted as the owner in the same shape.
  - **Family A2, by a device that dates each event a day before the last:** every event answers the same Skill wrongly on a day of its own. The first 6,000 go up through `familyDeviceA2`'s own upload, 60 batches of 100 sent back to back, and the rest are inserted as the owner.

  At each size the block times a one-event upload and `/api/play/family`, the median of three, and prints what it measured. A confirmed gap is an `it.fails` for the property that should hold beside a `today_…` test pinning what happens now.

- **Left to other scope issues:** a device forging Stars, Mastery or dates within its own Family, and the idempotency key's scope, are F3's (H10). That nothing limits how often a device uploads is H8, I2a's: the arithmetic below assumes no limit, as today.

## Where the figures come from

**Not from the E1 deployment.** This sandbox has no container runtime, so neither the gated run nor `pnpm pen-test:up` could run here. The figures below come from running the F4 block itself, unchanged, under vitest in this sandbox. It ran against PGlite 0.5.16 (Postgres 18.6 compiled to WebAssembly), served over the Postgres wire protocol by `@electric-sql/pglite-socket` in a separate process. It printed:

| Added events                          | One-event upload | `/api/play/family` |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| Ordinary play, Family A1: 1,000       | 25 ms            | 22 ms              |
| Ordinary play, Family A1: 10,000      | 161 ms           | 162 ms             |
| Ordinary play, Family A1: 100,000     | 1,468 ms         | 1,491 ms           |
| One day per event, Family A2: 1,000   | 159 ms           | 156 ms             |
| One day per event, Family A2: 6,000   | 6,670 ms         | 6,545 ms           |
| One day per event, Family A2: 10,000  | 18,646 ms        | 28,683 ms          |
| One day per event, Family A2: 100,000 | Not run          | Not run            |

The first 6,000 of Family A2's events took `familyDeviceA2` 134 s of uploads, the slowest 8.2 s. The fixtures' own play sits under each history: 40 events in Family A1 and 36 in Family A2.

How far these carry to the deployment:

- **The replay is JavaScript, and that is where the time goes.** `applyPlayRecords` alone, given the same histories in plain Node with no database, takes 141 ms at 100,000 events of ordinary play. With one day per event it takes 122 ms at 1,000, 2.2 s at 4,000 and 8.9 s at 8,000. The rest is reading the rows and the request around them. A faster Postgres shortens the ordinary figures somewhat and the day-per-event figures hardly at all.
- **The host's CPU is the multiplier.** A CPU twice as fast halves every figure. That moves each threshold below by a factor of two for ordinary play and by √2 for one day per event.
- **On the deployment**, the owner can repeat the measurement with the steps in the finding issues. The gated run on the orchestrator's Docker host prints its own figures as `pen-test.f4.family-requests.timed`, `pen-test.f4.history-uploads.timed` and `pen-test.f4.bystander-requests.timed`. Its pass or fail is the verdict's evidence, and these figures are its scale.

Wizzaroo sets no response-time target for its API. This note calls a request **slow** when it takes over a second, and **stalled** over ten.

The cost half of H10 is **confirmed**. Every upload and every opening of play re-reads and replays the Family's whole history, as documented, with nothing to bound it (#318). A device that dates its events a day apart makes that replay grow with the square of the history (#319). The replay holds the server's only thread, so one Family's history slows every Family (#320).

## Ordinary play: confirmed as a documented decision

At 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 events, a one-event upload took 25 ms, 161 ms and 1.47 s, and `/api/play/family` 22 ms, 162 ms and 1.49 s. Each is about 16 ms plus 14.5 µs for every event the Family has. An upload of one event costs as much as an opening because of the room showcases: storing it refreshes the Family's `room_showcases`, which replays all 100,000. No table holds a running total, and nothing summarises or archives old events.

- `today_ takes longer to answer an upload, and /api/play/family, at each of 1k, 10k and 100k events of ordinary play, and over ten times as long at 100k as at 1k`
- `it.fails`: `answers an upload, and /api/play/family, at 100k events of ordinary play within twice the time it takes at 1k`

That the server replays is documented. [ADR 0004](../../architectural-decision-record/0004-offline-play-records-events-the-server-decides.md) says "the server replays the events". [Mastery](../../guide/mastery.md) says it does so "whenever the dashboard asks". [Stars](../../guide/stars-items-and-rooms.md) says it replays "the Family's events in order of the device's time". Section 4 of `wizzaroo.md` says spaced repetition "matters over six years", so the history is meant to last six years. Nothing records how big it may grow, or when replaying all of it on every request has to stop. A documented decision with no recorded end is a finding under the PRD. Filed as #318 (low).

How long ordinary play takes to make a Family slow, at these figures:

| How the history grows                                              | To slow (about 68,000 events) | To stalled (about 690,000 events) |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| One Child answering 100 questions a day                            | About 1.9 years               | About 19 years                    |
| Two Children, 100 questions a day each                             | About 11 months               | About 9.5 years                   |
| One device uploading ordinary-looking batches of 100, back to back | About 6 minutes, 680 uploads  | About 9.6 hours, 6,900 uploads    |

One Child playing that much through Years 1 to 6 makes about 219,000 events, so every request their Family makes would take about 3.2 s by Year 6. Real play sits between the two histories probed here. Spaced repetition brings a Mastered Skill back on many days over the years, so a Skill gathers more days than ordinary play's ten. That costs more, as the next section shows.

## Dating each event a day apart: confirmed

The replay keeps, for each Child and Skill, one practice day per date (`daysBySkill`). For every event, `addPracticeDay` copies that Skill's whole list, combines it and sorts it again. `answerQuestion` then rebuilds the Skill's Mastery from the whole list, until the Skill is Mastered. So an event costs as much as its Skill has dates. The device chooses each event's `localDate`, any valid date (H10, ADR 0004), and wrong answers never reach Mastery. A history of wrong answers to one Skill, each on a day of its own, costs the square of its length. One upload of 100 such events is a schema-valid batch, and a Child could make it by changing the tablet's date. A tester makes it with `curl`.

At 1,000 events a one-event upload took 159 ms. At 6,000 it took 6.7 s, and at 10,000 18.6 s, with `/api/play/family` at 28.7 s. That is about 0.19 µs times the square of the history: 10 times the events, 117 times the time. 100,000 such events would take about 31 minutes per request. The probe didn't run that size, because it would have been load, not measurement.

- `today_ takes over thirty times as long to answer an upload, and /api/play/family, at 10k device-dated events as at 1k, growing with the square of the history`
- `it.fails`: `answers an upload, and /api/play/family, at 10k events within twice the time it takes at 1k, whatever dates the device gave them`

### How long one device takes, by arithmetic

The device needn't reach 100,000. Each upload adds at most 100 events, and nothing limits how often it uploads (H8). The time to reach a history of _n_ events by back-to-back uploads is the sum of what each upload costs on the way, about 0.19 µs × 100² × (1² + 2² + … + (_n_/100)²):

| Every one of the Family's requests takes | At             | Uploads of 100 | The device's time to get there |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Over a second (slow)                     | 2,325 events   | 24             | About 10 seconds               |
| Over ten seconds (stalled)               | 7,350 events   | 74             | About 4¼ minutes               |
| About 31 minutes                         | 100,000 events | 1,000          | About 7 days                   |

The block checks this arithmetic against the server. Sixty uploads, 6,000 events, took `familyDeviceA2` 134 s, and the formula gives 137 s. After them, its Family's next upload took 6.7 s and its next `/api/play/family` 6.5 s.

- `today_ lets familyDeviceA2, with 60 back-to-back uploads of 100 events taking under five minutes in all, make its Family's next upload and /api/play/family each take over a second`
- `it.fails`: `keeps its Family's upload and /api/play/family under a second after one Family Device's 60 uploads of 100 events`

> **Note, 18 November 2026 (#324):** the upload now sets aside any event dated more than 400 days before it is received, or later than tomorrow, so these 6,000 events, one date each going back about 16 years, can no longer all be stored through `POST /api/play/events`. The probe now writes each batch of 100 straight into Postgres and times a one-event upload after each, and its assertions are unchanged. The titles still say "60 uploads of 100 events", as filed. Through the API, the same attack is now held to about 402 dates per Skill per Child, which narrows this finding but doesn't fix it.

ADR 0004 decides that "Children altering the clock isn't treated as a threat", for what a Child could earn by it. It doesn't consider what the dates cost the server. A device's dates set the replay's work, nothing bounds it, and the PRD's check that "nothing is left to a collaborator to bound" fails. Filed as #319 (medium).

## Who "everyone" is: confirmed, past the Family

Within the Family, every request that replays the history waits for it:

| Route                                                         | Who sends it                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `POST /api/play/events`                                       | Each of the Family's devices, after each event and every minute                                                                                          |
| `GET /api/play/family`                                        | Each of the Family's devices, each time the game opens                                                                                                   |
| `GET /api/children`                                           | Each Parent, opening the dashboard                                                                                                                       |
| `GET /api/children/:childId/progress`                         | A Parent, or a Teacher or the Admin, for one of its Children                                                                                             |
| `PUT /api/children/:childId/classmate-visits`                 | A Parent                                                                                                                                                 |
| `GET /api/school-children`                                    | Each Teacher whose Year Groups hold one of its Children, and the Admin. It replays every Family in reach, so one long history slows the whole class list |
| `POST /api/admin/families/:familyId/family-chest-coin-awards` | The Admin                                                                                                                                                |

Those were read from the code, not timed. Past the Family, the server is one Node process: `src/server/index.ts` listens once, with no cluster and no worker. The replay is synchronous JavaScript, so nothing else runs while it does. The block timed `familyDeviceB`'s `/api/play/family`, Family B at School B, at 7 ms alone. It then sent the same request again and again while Family A2's 10,000-event `/api/play/family` was replayed. The slowest waited 18.7 s.

- `today_ keeps familyDeviceB's /api/play/family, at another School, waiting over a second, and over ten times as long as it takes alone, while Family A2's 10k events are worked out`
- `it.fails`: `answers familyDeviceB's /api/play/family within 200 ms of the time it takes alone while Family A2's events are worked out`

So the arithmetic above is for every Family on the server, not just one. Once a device's Family has 2,325 such events, each of its uploads holds every Family, School, Teacher and the Admin for over a second. At 7,350 it holds them for ten. A device that keeps uploading leaves the server's thread busy nearly all the time, and nothing ties the cost to the Family that made it. The upload also holds a database connection and an open transaction for the whole replay. Ordinary play reaches the same place more slowly: at 100,000 events each request holds everyone for about 1.5 s. Families are Wizzaroo's boundary ([ADR 0001](../../architectural-decision-record/0001-family-is-the-data-boundary.md), section 11 of `wizzaroo.md`), and nothing keeps one Family's cost out of another's requests. Filed as #320 (medium).

## Fixed, unverified

All three are fixed, unverified by a retest on the deployment, by [ADR 0055](../../architectural-decision-record/0055-the-server-keeps-each-familys-replay-and-picks-it-up.md). Each `it.fails` now passes as a plain `it`, and each `today_…` probe is a `(regression)` test.

- **#318:** the server keeps what each Family's replay works out, in memory, and a request replays only the records numbered since. Every play event, Star award and Family Chest coin award is numbered as it is written, under a lock on its Family's row, so a Family's records commit in the order of their numbers. Measured in the suite against Postgres 18: an upload and `/api/play/family` take 10 to 16 ms at 1k, 10k and 100k events of ordinary play, where they took 40 ms, 255 ms and 4.3 s before.
- **#319:** a Skill's practice days are kept so that adding one costs the same however many there are, and Mastery is worked out from the good days alone. At 1k, 6k and 10k device-dated events an upload takes about 15 to 18 ms and `/api/play/family` 11 to 14 ms, where they took 0.2 s, 4.7 s and 16 s. The 60 uploads take about 2 s in all, the slowest about 41 ms.
- **#320:** familyDeviceB's `/api/play/family` waits at most about 35 ms, against about 10 ms alone, while Family A2's 10k events are worked out, where it waited 13 s.

The 10k timing of the device-dated history is now the median of three, as at every other size. The probe timed one run there only because each took seconds. The first of the three is the one that replays the 4,000 events the probe inserts as the owner, behind the server's back, which uploads would have brought 100 at a time: about 120 ms, nearly all of it Postgres's row-level security checking each row read.
