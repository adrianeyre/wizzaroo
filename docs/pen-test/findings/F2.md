# F2: Picture Passwords as a non-boundary

> **Personal use only.** Wizzaroo is intended for your own personal use only. It is not intended for use with or by a school, teacher, parent or student. Only use it for yourself.

The findings note for scope issue #216 (F2) of [the first pen-test round](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md), answering the Picture Password half of [H9](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md#h9-a-family-device-credential-lives-for-ever-and-carries-every-picture-password-hash--medium). The credential half of H9 is F1's. This note names exploit chains, so it is kept private on the same terms as the PRD. How a finding is filed, triaged and verified is in [the findings workflow](../README.md).

- **Trust boundary:** [ADR 0005](../../architectural-decision-record/0005-family-devices-and-picture-passwords.md) makes the Family Device credential the boundary. A Picture Password keeps siblings apart on a device and is not a boundary. Section 8 of [`wizzaroo.md`](../../product-requirements-document/wizzaroo.md#8-accounts-and-roles) says the same, and that "Children play at home only; there are no School devices". The PRD puts Picture Passwords out of scope as a boundary, and puts in scope "a Picture Password hash that escapes the device".
- **Code:** `src/server/children/picture-password.ts` makes and checks the hash. `GET /api/play/family` in `src/server/family-devices/family-device-routes.ts` sends it, and `src/client/family-device/playable-family.ts` keeps it in `localStorage`. `PUT /api/children/:childId/picture-password` (`src/server/children/children-routes.ts`) and `PUT /api/play/children/:childId/picture-password` set it. The export is `src/server/family-data/family-export.ts`, and Classmate visits are `src/server/family-devices/classmate-room-routes.ts`. The column is `children.picture_password_hash`, granted to `wizzaroo_reach` with the rest of `children` (`src/server/drizzle/0018_row-level-security.sql`).
- **Documented behaviour:** [Picture Passwords](../../guide/children.md) ("The dashboard never receives the hash"), [Family Devices](../../guide/family-devices.md) (what a device fetches and keeps) and [the export](../../guide/family-data-and-admin.md) ("Passwords, Picture Password hashes … are left out").
- **Probes:** the `pen test F2: Picture Passwords as a non-boundary` block in `src/integration.test.ts`, in the gated run against a real Postgres. It loads [the fixtures](../fixtures.md) into a database of its own, `pen_test_f2`, and loads them again before every probe. Adults sign in through the credentials-package harness, and Family Devices send their fixture token.

So H9's Picture Password half is **confirmed as a documented decision**. Every Child's hash goes to their own Family's devices and stays there, and a holder of the device finds any Child's Picture Password at once, as ADR 0005 accepts. Through the API the hash goes nowhere else: not to another Family, a Classmate's Room visit, an export, the Audit Log or any adult. Behind the API, Postgres lets a Teacher's reach and the Admin's reach read it (F2-1). ADR 0005 records no condition under which its decision stops holding (F2-2).

## On the device: confirmed as a documented decision

**What a device is sent (probe 1).** `GET /api/play/family` answers each active device with `picturePasswordHash` for every Child in its Family, exactly as stored. `familyDeviceA1` gets Ada's and Ben's, `familyDeviceA2` Cara's and Dev's, and `familyDeviceB` Eli's. No device gets another Family's. The client keeps the whole reply in `localStorage` under `wizzaroo.family-device.playable-family` and checks a Picture Password against it with no network. That is the documented design.

- `on the device, as ADR 0005 decides › sends each Family Device every Picture Password hash in its own Family, exactly as stored, and none from another Family`

**The offline search (probe 2).** A Picture Password is 3 pictures from 9, repeats allowed, so there are 9³ = 729. The hash is `pbkdf2-sha256$100000$<salt>$<key>`, salted per Child, so each Child is a search of their own. The probe takes the two hashes `familyDeviceA1` is sent and tries all 729 combinations against each with the client's own `isPicturePasswordRight`. Exactly one matches for each, and it is that Child's fixture Picture Password.

- `on the device, as ADR 0005 decides › gives up Ada's and Ben's Picture Passwords to a search of all 729 combinations against the hashes familyDeviceA1 is sent`

How long it takes, measured in the sandbox this probe was written in (Node 26.10.0, WebCrypto PBKDF2 on four libuv threads): one check takes about 8.7 ms, and all 729 for one Child take about 1.6 s. The PBKDF2 cost slows a search by a factor of 100,000 per guess, which is no protection with only 729 guesses. Anyone holding the device, or a copy of its `localStorage`, has every Child's Picture Password in seconds. ADR 0005 accepts that: a sibling can open a sibling's Character, and nothing beyond that Family's play is behind a Picture Password.

**The check isn't constant-time.** Read, not probed. `isPicturePasswordRight` compares hex strings with `===`. The comparison runs only in the browser, and the server never checks a Picture Password, so there is no remote timing to measure. Anyone who could time it on the device already holds the hash. This is part of the documented decision, not a finding of its own.

## Nowhere else, through the API: refuted

**Another Family (probe 3).** No device is sent another Family's hash (probe 1, and the sweep below). In Postgres, each device's reach reads its own Family's hashes and no other Family's, a Classmate's included.

- `in Postgres, behind the API › keeps a Family Device's reach from reading another Family's Picture Password hash, a Classmate's included`

**A Classmate's Room visit (probe 4).** Ada and Cara are in Year 3 at School A, which approves visits, and both have visits on. `familyDeviceA1` asks for Ada's Classmates' Rooms and gets one Room, Cara's, with no hash. `classmate-room-routes.ts` selects only `loadout`, `room` and `trophies` from `room_showcases`, and the reply carries no Child id. Within `familyDeviceA1`'s reach, `room_showcases` holds Cara's row, while `children` holds only Ada and Ben. `wizzaroo_can_visit_classmate_room` is `SECURITY DEFINER`, but it returns a boolean, so it reads Cara's row without handing any of it back.

- `nowhere else, through the API › sends Ada's Family Device Cara's Room on a Classmate visit with no Picture Password hash, and its reach can read Cara's Room but not her row`

**The export (probe 5).** `parentA1`'s `GET /api/family-export` lists Ada and Ben, and holds neither hash nor a `picturePasswordHash` field. `loadFamilyExport` names each column it exports, and the column isn't among them.

- `nowhere else, through the API › leaves every Picture Password hash out of a Family's data export, its own Children's included`

**The Audit Log (probe 6).** `parentA1` sets Ada's Picture Password on the dashboard. `familyDeviceA2` sets Cara's first one after the probe clears it, as for a Child a School brought in. Both answer `204`, both new hashes check against the pictures sent, and each writes a `picture-password.set` entry naming the Child. No entry holds a hash, the hash mark `pbkdf2-sha256$` or a picture's name.

- `nowhere else, through the API › records a Picture Password set by a Parent or by a Family Device in the Audit Log with neither its hash nor its pictures`

**Every other reply (probe 7).** The sweep signs in every fixture identity in the credentials package: nine adults and three active devices, plus nobody. `teacherADeactivated`, `coParentBInviteExpired` and `familyDeviceA1Revoked` are refused at sign-in, as they should be. Each identity then sends every `GET` route the server registers, 22 of them, with each `:childId` filled in with all five fixture Children. It also sends the six writes D2 found that reply with a Child, aimed at Ada with the values she already has. That is 36 requests per identity and 468 in all, of which 128 are served (status below 300). A reply counts as carrying a hash if it holds any fixture Child's derived key or the mark `pbkdf2-sha256$`. Only three replies do, and each is `GET /api/play/family` to a device, carrying its own Family's hashes. Among the replies with none are the Parent dashboard's `/api/children` and `/api/family`, the Teacher's `/api/school-children`, the Admin's `/api/admin/families`, `/api/parents` and `/api/admin/audit-log-entries`, every Child's progress, and the export. `child-progress.ts` sends `hasPicturePassword`, a boolean, and nothing more.

- `nowhere else, through the API › sends a Picture Password hash in no reply to any fixture identity, signed in or not, on any GET route or any write that replies with a Child, but /api/play/family to its own Family's devices`

The routes swept are listed from the server at run time (`listServerRoutes`), so a `GET` route added later is swept too. Writes are swept only where D2 found they reply with an existing Child. Read, not swept: the routes that add a Child (`POST /api/children` and `POST /api/families/:familyId/children`) reply through the same `loadChild`, whose `childColumns` select `hasPicturePassword` and not the hash. No `select()` or `returning()` in `src/server` takes a whole row.

## In Postgres, behind the API: confirmed

**A Teacher's and the Admin's reach read the hash (probe 8).** `wizzaroo_reach` holds `SELECT` on every column of `children`, and the policies decide only which rows. So each reach reads `picture_password_hash` for every Child it can see:

| Reach                | Hashes it reads   |
| -------------------- | ----------------- |
| `parentA1`           | Ada, Ben          |
| `parentB`            | Eli               |
| `teacherAYear3`      | Ada, Cara, Dev    |
| `teacherAYears5And6` | Ben               |
| `teacherBYear3`      | Eli               |
| `admin`              | All five Children |
| `familyDeviceA1`     | Ada, Ben          |
| `familyDeviceA2`     | Cara, Dev         |
| `familyDeviceB`      | Eli               |

A Family's devices need the hash, by ADR 0005. A Parent can sign a device in and be sent it anyway, so their reach reading it gives them nothing new. A Teacher or the Admin has no device and no route that serves the hash, and the guide says "the dashboard never receives the hash". So keeping it from them is enforced in the API alone. A staff route that selected the whole row, or any bug on a Teacher's or the Admin's reach, would send them every hash they can see, and with probe 2, every Picture Password. This is H5's shape, rows and not columns, for a read. Since a Picture Password guards nothing but which sibling plays, it is filed as F2-1 (low).

_Fixed, unverified (#304):_ migration 0055 takes `SELECT` on `picture_password_hash` from `wizzaroo_reach` and grants it every other column of `children`, so no reach reads the hash, a Parent's included. A Family Device's reach is handed its own Family's hashes by `wizzaroo_family_device_picture_password_hash`, a `SECURITY DEFINER` function that answers only a reach naming no adult, for a Child of that reach's Family. `GET /api/play/family` reads them through it. Whether a Child has a Picture Password is a generated column, `has_picture_password`, which staff's and Parents' reaches read instead, and the first-Picture-Password route checks it.

- `in Postgres, behind the API › refuses every reach the Picture Password hash column, so only a Family Device's reach reads a hash, its own Family's, through the function that hands it over (regression)` (the `today_` probe)
- `in Postgres, behind the API › withholds every Picture Password hash from a Teacher's reach and the Admin's` (the `it.fails`, now passing)

## The documented decision has no condition for ending it

ADR 0005 decides that the Picture Password is not a boundary, and the round confirms that the code does what it says. The PRD counts "a documented decision with no recorded expiry" as a low finding. ADR 0005 records no expiry, and no condition under which it would stop holding. The decision holds because:

- a Family Device is used by one Family's Children at home;
- a Picture Password opens one Child's play and nothing more.

Section 8 of `wizzaroo.md` says "there are no School devices", but ADR 0005 doesn't tie its decision to that, or to anything else. Schools, Teachers and Classmates have arrived since it was written. A School or classroom device, a device shared between Families, or a Picture Password guarding something a sibling mustn't reach would each make the hash on the device a boundary that falls in seconds (probe 2). Nothing would prompt anyone to revisit ADR 0005 when that happens. Filed as F2-2 (low). No test can catch a missing record, so its fix is a written condition, beside probes 1 and 2, which turn red if the behaviour changes.

_Fixed, unverified (#305):_ [ADR 0005](../../architectural-decision-record/0005-family-devices-and-picture-passwords.md) now names the two conditions its decision rests on, lists the changes that revisit it (a School or classroom device, a device shared between Families, a Picture Password guarding a purchase, a setting or anything a Parent decides, or anything a sibling mustn't reach), and sets a review date, 31 March 2027, in the style of ADR 0049. Probes 1 and 2 stay as they are.

## Read, not probed

- **The hash after a device is revoked.** The client deletes its stored Family only when `/api/play/family` answers `401`. A revoked device that stays offline keeps every hash until it next reaches the server. That is the device credential's lifetime, so it's F1's (H9's credential half).
- **The Picture Password in transit.** Setting one sends the three pictures to the server in a `PUT` body, which hashes them before opening the transaction. Fastify's logger records no request bodies, and the routes log only ids. If a query failed on the update, the unhandled error's message would carry the new hash among its parameters, in the reply and the log. That is H7, I1's, and wasn't probed here.
