# B3: Admin reach and bulk creation by CSV

> **Personal use only.** Wizzaroo is intended for your own personal use only. It is not intended for use with or by a school, teacher, parent or student. Only use it for yourself.

The findings note for scope issue #208 (B3). It answers the Admin parts of [H6](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md#h6-some-admin-reads-are-not-logged-and-the-log-can-be-changed--high) (with D2) and [H14](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md#h14-deletion-leaves-copies-behind--medium), and the CSV clause of [H19](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md#h19-query-construction-is-clean-sweep-it-anyway--low). It also confirms one weakness the opening register did not anticipate. It names an exploit chain, so it is kept private on the same terms as the PRD ([the findings workflow](../README.md)).

## The rules probed

- **The Admin** runs Wizzaroo itself and reaches every School, Teacher, Parent and Child. "Each time the Admin opens a Child's progress, it is logged" (section 8 of [`wizzaroo.md`](../../product-requirements-document/wizzaroo.md)). "Logged Admin access" is an item on the second-School gate (section 11). [ADR 0041](../../architectural-decision-record/0041-security-actions-are-kept-in-an-audit-log.md) lists the security actions the Audit Log keeps.
- **Deletion** of a Child or a Family is "part of the data deletion flow" (section 8). [Your Family's data and the Admin dashboard](../../guide/family-data-and-admin.md) says the Admin "can delete a Child or a Family directly too".
- **Bulk creation** (section 8, "Bulk creation"):
  - a Teacher's upload is limited to their own School and their Year Groups;
  - an existing Parent's email adds the new Child to that Parent's Family;
  - a likely duplicate is flagged and skipped;
  - invalid rows come back in a skipped-rows report;
  - Invite emails go out in batches, to stay within the provider's limits.
- **Teacher reach** is read-only ([ADR 0026](../../architectural-decision-record/0026-schools-and-teachers.md), [ADR 0027](../../architectural-decision-record/0027-row-level-security-through-a-reach-role.md)). Adding a Parent to a Family one at a time, `POST /api/parents`, needs a Parent of that Family within the Teacher's reach (`assertFamilySeen`, `src/server/people/people-routes.ts`).

## Verdicts

1. **Every Admin route: what it reads, what it changes, whether it is logged.** **Confirmed.** 20 routes refuse everyone but the Admin. Every Admin change in ADR 0041's list writes its entry. But three routes serve the Admin Children's data with no access log row, eight Admin changes to other Families' Children and Schools write no Audit Log entry, and deleting a Child erases the access log rows that were written. Findings: #287, #288; both fixed, unverified.
2. **Admin deletion of a Family or Child without a deletion request.** **Confirmed as a documented decision.** It works, it is in the Audit Log, and no Parent is told. Nothing records when the decision has to be looked at again. Finding: #290; fixed, unverified, with #284: the Admin now deletes only on a Parent's request.
3. **Formula injection in the skipped-rows report.** **Refuted.** No cell of the report starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return, whatever the upload held.
4. **A Teacher's upload reaching outside their School or Year Groups.** **Confirmed.** The School column is ignored, and a Child outside the Teacher's Year Groups or a Parent at another School is skipped. But a row that names any Parent at the Teacher's own School adds a Child, or a new adult, to that Parent's Family, even when none of its Children is in the Teacher's Year Groups. The new adult is emailed an Invite and gets full Parent reach over the whole Family. Finding: #286; fixed, unverified, by #265's upload check, with #277.
5. **Duplicate and existing-Parent handling.** **Refuted** for what the rules name: a likely duplicate, a Child twice in one file, a taken Teacher email and a staff email are all skipped, and an existing Parent gets no new account or Invite. **Confirmed** for what the preview tells a Teacher: whether an address belongs to a Parent at another School, or to staff. Finding: #291; fixed, unverified.
6. **The length cap.** **Confirmed.** The million-character cap and the thousand-row cap both hold. Neither bounds the work: every column of every row is held, and rows are counted only after all of them are read. Finding: #289; fixed, unverified.
7. **The batch of Invite emails.** **Confirmed.** Each upload sends its own batches, so two uploads at once send twice the batch. One failed email abandons the rest of its upload, after the reply has counted them all as emailed. Findings: #292, #293; both fixed, unverified.

Severities, from the PRD's table:

- #286 is **critical**: a Teacher can read and change Children outside their Year Groups, the separation the round exists to prove.
- #287 is **high**: Admin access to Children's data goes unlogged.
- #288 and #289 are **medium**: a control is weaker than its documentation says.
- The other four are **low**: one is a documented decision with no recorded expiry, and three are disclosure and hygiene.

Every Admin change in ADR 0041's list writes exactly the entries it names, in the Admin's name. That includes a confirmed deletion request, which writes two. That part is **refuted**. The gaps are what the list leaves out, and the reads.

**Reads.** `GET /api/children/:childId/progress` writes one row per read it serves and none for a refused one, inside the transaction and before the read. `GET /api/school-children` serves every Child's progress with no row. `GET /api/admin/families` and `GET /api/parents` serve every Child's nickname with no row. `admin_access_logs.child_id` cascades on delete, so when the Admin deletes a Child directly, every row recording that the Admin opened that Child's progress goes too. The change and erasure of rows through the reach role's grants is H6's third bullet and D2's. _Fixed, unverified (#287)._ The three reads were already logged by D2's fix (#267, #268). `admin_access_logs.child_id` is no longer a foreign key (migration 0052), so a Child's rows outlive their deletion, or their Family's, holding the bare id, and go with the daily purge once older than the Audit Log's twelve months.

**Changes.** These Admin changes to a Family that isn't the Admin's, or to a School, write nothing to the Audit Log and nothing the Family can see:

- Stars;
- Family Chest coins;
- a School's name;
- a Child's details, limits, Teacher locks, Classmate visits and birth.

Only the container log has them, and for a Child's details only the Child's id. ADR 0041's list doesn't name them, but the gate's "logged Admin access" does, and a Parent can't tell the Admin changed their Child. _Fixed, unverified (#288)._ Each now writes one entry in the Admin's name, naming the Child, Family or School and keeping no value: `child.stars-awarded`, `family-chest.coins-awarded`, `school.changed`, `child.changed`, `child.limits-changed`, `child.teacher-locks-changed`, `child.classmate-visits-changed` and `child.birth-given`. The Admin's changes to their own Family's Child are a Parent's, and write none.

Read from the code, not probed: the Admin's first Invite for a new Teacher or Parent is covered by `teacher.created` or `parent.created`, and no `invite.sent` is written until a resend. That belongs to D6's completeness sweep (`notes.md`).

- `it.fails`: `writes an access log row for a Child each time one of those routes serves the Admin her data`
- Passing: `writes exactly one access log row each time the Admin opens a Child's progress, and none for a Child who isn't there`

## Probe 2: deleting with no deletion request

1. As the Admin, `DELETE /api/admin/children/e2000000-0000-4000-8000-c10000000002` (Ben, Family A1): 204.
2. `DELETE /api/admin/families/e2000000-0000-4000-8000-fa0000000002` (Family A2): 204.
3. Neither Family has a deletion request. Ben, Cara and Dev are gone, and so are the accounts of Family A2's two Parents.
4. The Audit Log has `child.deleted` and `family.deleted` in the Admin's name. No email goes to any Parent of either Family.

This is what the guide documents, so it is **confirmed as a documented decision**. Nothing records why the Admin needs it, or when it should be looked at again. A deletion the Family never asked for is told to no one, and it can't be undone short of a restore. Combined with H11, a page on another `adrianeyre.co.uk` subdomain could send the bodiless `DELETE` with the Admin's cookie; that is A1's to probe.

_Fixed, unverified (#290, with #284)._ The owner's decision is that the Admin deletes only what a Parent asked to delete. Both `DELETE` routes now answer 409 "A Parent of this Family has to ask for this deletion first" unless a matching deletion request is waiting, and with one they confirm it as `POST /api/admin/deletion-requests/:deletionRequestId/confirmation` does, writing `deletion-request.confirmed` beside `child.deleted` or `family.deleted`. The Admin dashboard no longer offers a deletion beside each Family and Child. The Parent who asked is the Family's notice, so no email was added.

Deleting a Child directly also erases the access log rows for that Child (probe 1). So the Admin can open a Child's progress, delete the Child, and leave only `child.deleted` behind. That is filed under #287.

- Documented, passing: `deletes a Child and a Family with no deletion request, as documented, recording each in the Audit Log and telling no Parent`
- `today_ erases the record that the Admin opened a Child's progress when the Admin then deletes that Child directly`
- `it.fails`: `keeps the record that the Admin opened a Child's progress after the Admin deletes that Child`

## Probe 3: formula injection in the skipped-rows report

The report (`buildSkippedRowsReport`, `src/server/bulk/bulk-rows.ts`) has two columns: the row number, and the problem. Every problem starts with fixed text or with a literal `"` before the echoed cell, as in `"=1+1" isn't a birth month from 1 to 12`. Spreadsheets read a leading quote as text. The row's summary, which starts with the typed name, goes only into the JSON preview, which React renders as text. The client downloads the server's report unchanged (`src/client/staff/bulk-upload-panel.tsx`).

The probe uploads `=HYPERLINK(…)`, `+1+1`, `-1+1`, `@SUM(1)`, a leading tab and a leading carriage return into every cell a problem echoes:

- Teachers: email, School and Year Groups;
- Families: School, birth month, birth year and Parent email.

All 42 rows are skipped, the report holds the hostile text, and no cell starts with a formula character. Leading tabs and carriage returns are trimmed when the file is read. **Refuted.**

- Passing: `writes the skipped-rows report so that no cell starts with a character a spreadsheet reads as a formula, whatever the upload held`

## Probe 4: a Teacher's upload reaching outside their School or Year Groups

What holds:

- A Teacher's file has no School column. If it has one, it is ignored, and every row goes to the Teacher's School.
- A Child whose worked-out Year isn't one of the Teacher's Year Groups is skipped.
- A row naming a Parent at another School is skipped: "already a Parent at another School, so the Admin adds this Child".

What doesn't: `previewFamilies` (`src/server/bulk/bulk-families.ts`) accepts an existing Parent when they are within the Teacher's reach **or when they belong to the Teacher's School at all**:

```ts
seenParentIds !== undefined &&
  !seenParentIds.has(existing.userId) &&
  !existing.schoolIds.includes(school.id);
```

Every Parent is linked to the School that invited them. So any Parent at the Teacher's School passes, whether or not any of their Children is in the Teacher's Year Groups. The Family is then written as the database owner, so row-level security never sees it.

Steps, as `teacherAYears5And6` (School A, Years 5 and 6). Family A2's Children, Cara and Dev, are both in Year 3, so outside this Teacher's reach:

1. `POST /api/parents` with `{"name": "B3 Intruder", "email": "b3-one-at-a-time-…@pen-test.example", "familyId": "e2000000-0000-4000-8000-fa0000000002", "invite": "link"}`: **404** "There's no such Family within your reach". One at a time, the rule holds.
2. `POST /api/bulk/families` with `sendInvites: true` and this file. Row 2 is a Child in Year 5 and row 3 has no Child. Both name `parentA2PasskeyOnly`'s address, so they are one Family:

   ```csv
   child_name,birth_month,birth_year,year_group_adjustment,parent_1_name,parent_1_email,parent_2_name,parent_2_email
   Zed…,3,<a Year 5 birth year>,,,<parentA2PasskeyOnly's email>,,
   ,,,,,<parentA2PasskeyOnly's email>,B3 Intruder,b3-intruder-…@pen-test.example
   ```

   **201**: `createdRowCount: 2`, `skippedRows: []`, `invitesEmailedCount: 1`. Zed is a Child of Family A2. `b3-intruder-…` is a Parent of Family A2, emailed an Invite naming School A.

3. Accept the Invite from that mailbox: 204 and a Parent sign-in. A Parent needs no second factor.
4. `GET /api/children`: **200** with Cara, Dev and Zed, and their progress and limits.
5. `PUT /api/children/<Cara>/limits`: **200**. The adult can also make a Family Device, download the Family's export, and ask for the Family's deletion.
6. Nothing is emailed to Family A2's Parents.

So a Teacher's read-only reach over Years 5 and 6 becomes a Parent's write reach over any Family that has a Parent at their School. That is every Family at the School. Two variants, read from the code:

- **No new adult, then one at a time.** The Year 5 row alone is enough. Once Zed is in Family A2, the Family is in the Teacher's reach, so `POST /api/parents` with its `familyId` passes `assertFamilySeen`, and a copy-link Invite goes to whoever the Teacher likes.
- **Within reach, by design.** For a Family the Teacher does see, adding a second adult is documented ("A second adult in a household is added by a Teacher or the Admin"). That gives the same Parent reach over that Family's other Children, outside the Teacher's Year Groups. It isn't this probe's, and it is in `notes.md` for B2 and A4.

Fixed, unverified (#286, with #265 and #277). A Teacher's row naming an existing Parent outside their reach is skipped, whatever School the Parent is at. Both rows of the file above are skipped, so no Child, adult or Invite is made. Since #291 the reason is "Someone in Wizzaroo already uses Parent 1's email, so the Admin adds this row", the one every address taken outside the Teacher's reach gets.

- `skips a Teacher of Years 5 and 6's rows naming a Parent at their School whose Children are all outside their Year Groups, so no Child or new adult joins that Family and no Invite is emailed (regression)`, which was `today_ lets a Teacher of Years 5 and 6 add a Child, and a new adult who is emailed an Invite, …`
- `skips a Teacher's row that names a Parent outside their reach, as adding a Parent to that Family one at a time is refused`, which was `it.fails`
- Refuted, passing: `keeps a Teacher's upload to their own School and Year Groups: a School column is ignored, and a Child outside their Year Groups or a Parent at another School is skipped`

## Probe 5: duplicates and existing Parents

As the Admin, one Families upload of five rows:

- Ada again, under her co-Parent's email: skipped as a likely duplicate.
- A new sibling under `parentA1`'s email: added to Family A1. No second account, and no Invite to `parentA1`.
- The same new Child twice under one new Parent: the second row is skipped as a duplicate, and the first with it, since siblings are never split.
- A row whose Parent email is `teacherAYear3`'s: skipped, "belongs to someone who isn't a Parent".

The Teachers upload skips a taken email, and a repeat of an earlier row's email in capitals. All as section 8 says: **refuted**.

The duplicate check matches nickname (ignoring case), birth month and birth year only. It misses a Child the Family hasn't yet given a birth month and year, and a nickname typed with different spacing or look-alike letters. That is a usability edge, not a boundary, so it isn't filed.

What a Teacher learns from the preview is **confirmed**. As `teacherBYear3` (School B), previewing three Parent-only rows gives:

| Address                              | Problem                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `parentA1`'s, a Parent at School A   | "Parent 1 is already a Parent at another School, so the Admin adds this Child" |

- Refuted, passing: `adds an existing Parent's new Child to their Family with no new account or Invite, and skips a likely duplicate, a Child twice in the file and a staff member's email`
- Refuted, passing: `skips a Teacher whose email someone already uses, or that an earlier row has in another case`
- `answers a Teacher's preview alike for a Parent at another School, staff and an address no one has, so it says nothing of whose an address is (regression)`, which was `today_ tells a Teacher, in the preview, whether an email address belongs to a Parent at another School or to staff, …`

Fixed, unverified (#291, with #278). For a Teacher, every address with an account outside their reach, a Parent at another School, a Parent at their own School outside their Year Groups, or staff, gets one answer. Their preview treats it as an address no one has, so all three rows above pass it alike. The upload then skips the row with "Someone in Wizzaroo already uses Parent 1's email, so the Admin adds this row", which names no role and no School. "The two Parents are already in different Families" and the duplicate check now run only for Parents in the Teacher's reach, whose Families they see. The Admin's preview keeps its precise answers.

## Probe 6: the length cap

- A file of 1,000,001 characters is refused by the body schema with 400 before anything is read.
- A file of 1,001 rows is refused with 400 "Upload at most 1000 rows at a time", and no one is created.

Both caps hold. Neither bounds the work:

- `readCsvRecords` (`src/server/bulk/csv.ts`) makes a `Map` of **every heading for every row**, whether or not the template names it.
- `assertFewEnoughRows` runs only after every row has been read, so a file of 250,000 rows is read in full before it is refused.

Measured in a local Node process, `readCsvRecords` alone, a thousand rows of `x`:

| Columns | File  | Cells held   | Heap    | Time   |
| ------- | ----- | ------------ | ------- | ------ |
| 1,004   | 6 kB  | 1.0 million  | 45 MiB  | 47 ms  |
| 4,004   | 21 kB | 4.0 million  | 121 MiB | 160 ms |
| 16,004  | 81 kB | 16.0 million | 462 MiB | 687 ms |

That is about 30 bytes and 43 ns a cell. By arithmetic, within the caps:

- **A thousand rows:** about 166,000 headings fit in the rest of a million characters. That is 166 million cells, about **5 GB** of heap and 7 s of a blocked event loop. It passes the row cap.
- **Refused for too many rows:** 83,000 headings and 250,000 rows fit. That is 2 × 10¹⁰ cells, about **600 GB**, which is never reached because the process runs out of heap first.

Node's default heap limit is a few GB at most, set by the machine's memory. So one request from any Teacher, or the Admin, can end the app process and take Wizzaroo down for every Family until it restarts. Exercising that is out of scope, so it was worked out by arithmetic and not tried.

The probe pins the shape at a size that is safe to run: 4,004 columns and 1,000 rows, 28,697 characters, gives 4,004,000 cells and a 200 preview.

- Passing: `refuses an upload over a million characters, and one over a thousand rows, creating no one`
- `reads no row of a file within both caps whose heading row is wider than its template, and keeps only the template's columns of a file that isn't (regression)`, which was `today_ reads every column of every row in a file within both caps, …`
- `refuses a file with more columns than its template, so the caps bound what one upload holds`, which was `it.fails`

Fixed, unverified (#289). `readCsvRecords` reads the file a row at a time. A heading row with more columns than the template is refused with 400 before any data row is read; a Teacher's Families file may keep the Admin's School column, which is ignored. Only the template's columns are kept for each row, and reading stops with "Upload at most 1000 rows at a time" at row 1,001, so a refused file is never read in full. One upload now holds at most 1,000 rows of the template's columns.

## Probe 7: the batch of Invite emails

What holds:

- `POST /api/bulk/families` with `sendInvites: true` replies first.
- Then it emails each new Parent one Invite, `batchSize` at a time, with a pause between batches.
- An existing Parent gets no Invite.

What doesn't:

- **Batches are per upload.** `sendInviteEmailsLater` (`src/server/bulk/bulk-routes.ts`) starts its own `sendEmailsInBatches` loop for each upload. Two uploads at once have both first batches in flight together, four emails against a batch of two. With N uploads at once, Wizzaroo sends N times the rate the batching was set for. Each upload can hold 1,000 rows of two Parents, and nothing limits uploads (H8). A Teacher can therefore have Wizzaroo email up to 2,000 addresses of their choosing per request, with a greeting naming whoever they typed. The provider's limit is the whole deployment's. Breaking it risks the account that sends every reset and security notice.
- **One failure abandons the rest.** `sendEmailsInBatches` awaits `Promise.all` for each batch, so one refused address rejects the batch and ends the loop. The upload's other batches are never sent. The only trace is `bulk.invites.failed` in the container log. The reply has already said `invitesEmailedCount: 6`, the uploader is told nothing, and the Parents list shows each Invite as pending as if it had gone.

Tests:

- Passing: `emails each new Parent in an upload one Invite after the reply, a batch at a time, and no existing Parent`
- `puts two uploads' Invites in one queue, so one batch goes to the provider at a time and the second upload's wait their turn (regression)`, which was `today_ lets two uploads email their Invites side by side, …`
- `keeps Invite emails to one batch at a time across every upload, since the provider's limit is the deployment's and not one upload's`, which was `it.fails`
- `emails every other Invite in an upload once one email fails, and the Parents list shows that one Invite as not emailed, so the uploader sends a new one (regression)`, which was `today_ abandons the rest of an upload's Invites once one email fails, …`
- `still emails every other Invite in an upload when one email fails`, which was `it.fails`

Fixed, unverified (#292). Every upload's Invite emails join one queue per server, `buildEmailQueue` (`src/server/email/send-email.ts`), which keeps at most one batch in flight and pauses after each, however many uploads add to it.

Fixed, unverified (#293). Each email in a batch is settled on its own, so one that fails doesn't stop the rest. It is logged as `bulk.invites.failed` with its Invite's id, and its Invite is marked (`invites.email_failed_at`, migration 0051, written as the server, not within a reach). The Parents and Teachers lists then show that Invite as "Invite email not sent", with "Send … a new Invite" beside it. The reply's `invitesEmailedCount` still counts the Invites queued, since the emails go out after it.
