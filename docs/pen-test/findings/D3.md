# D3: export and deletion, logs and backups

> **Personal use only.** Wizzaroo is intended for your own personal use only. It is not intended for use with or by a school, teacher, parent or student. Only use it for yourself.

The findings note for scope issue #212 (D3) of [the first pen-test round](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md), which answers [H14](../../product-requirements-document/pen-testing.md#h14-deletion-leaves-copies-behind--medium). It names exploit chains, so it is kept private on the same terms as the PRD.

- **Trust boundary:** what leaves Wizzaroo about a Family when a Parent downloads it, and what is left of a Child or a Family once the Admin deletes it. Section 11 of `wizzaroo.md` makes "data export and deletion for Parents" and "nightly off-site backups" items on the second-School gate ([ADR 0026](../../architectural-decision-record/0026-schools-and-teachers.md)). [Your Family's data](../../guide/family-data-and-admin.md) promises that once the Admin confirms, "no row about them remains".
- **Code:** `src/server/family-data/family-export.ts`, `family-deletion.ts` and `family-data-routes.ts`, the deletion routes in `src/server/admin/admin-routes.ts`, the policies in `src/server/drizzle/0018_row-level-security.sql` and `0019_deletion-requests.sql`, `logger: true` in `src/server/http-server.ts`, `backup/backup.sh` and `restore.sh`, `docker-compose.yml`, and the device's outbox in `src/client/family-device/play-event-outbox.ts`.
- **Probes:** the `pen test D3: export and deletion, logs and backups` block in `src/integration.test.ts`, and the `pen test D3: a deleted Family and the nightly backups` block in `src/backup-restore.test.ts`, both in the gated run against a real Postgres. The first loads [the fixtures](../fixtures.md) into a database of its own and loads them again before every probe, clearing that database's Audit Log and any deletion request a probe made. It reads the server's log by watching what the server writes to standard output, which is what Docker keeps as the container log. The second loads the fixtures, backs them up with the real `backup.sh` to a scratch directory standing in for the NAS and another standing in for the bucket, has the Admin confirm Family B's deletion through the API, backs up again, and restores with the real `restore.sh` into empty databases. A confirmed gap is an `it.fails` for the property that should hold beside a `today_…` test pinning what happens now. A refuted probe is a passing test.
- **Left to other scope issues:** the failing SQL in a 500's _response body_ is H7, I1's. CSRF on the Admin's bodiless deletion confirmation is already #247 (A1). Column-level gaps in the policies are H5, D1's; the deletion gap below is filed here because who can delete is this issue's question.

## Export

`GET /api/family-export` runs in the downloading Parent's reach, as a Parent even for the Admin, and names every column it returns.

**Complete for the Family: refuted for each table it lists.** For `parentA1` and `parentB`, the export's Family, Parents, Children, Characters, Family Devices (the revoked one included), play events and Invites are exactly the fixture plan's, and Family B's waiting deletion request is there.

- `exports every row the plan gives a Family in each table it lists, for parentA1 and for parentB`

**Complete for the Parent: confirmed for the Audit Log.** The probe reads every row of every table in `public` as the database owner and lists the tables holding a row that names Family A1: its id, name, Parents' ids and email addresses, Children's ids, nicknames and Character names, and its devices', Invites' and play events' ids. Every table on that list is in the export but `audit_log_entries`. After `coParentA1` signs in with a password, the Audit Log holds their `sign-in.succeeded` entry and the IP address it came from (`CF-Connecting-IP`). Their export has neither. An IP address is personal data. The guide says the export has "a list for every table holding a Child's or an adult's data". [ADR 0041](../../architectural-decision-record/0041-security-actions-are-kept-in-an-audit-log.md) contrasts `admin_access_logs`, which "goes in the Family's export", with the Audit Log, but it never decides to leave the Audit Log out. Filed as #285 (low). _Fixed, unverified (#285)._ The export now has an `auditLogEntries` list: the downloading adult's own entries, taken by them or about them, with the IP address a sign-in came from. An entry another adult took about them keeps that adult's role and leaves out who they were.

- `today_ leaves the Audit Log out of the export, so coParentA1's own sign-in there, with the IP address it came from, isn't in their file`
- `it.fails`: `exports a list for every table holding a row that names the Parent or their Family, the Audit Log and its IP addresses included`

The export also leaves out who did things to the Family. Who sent each Invite (`invites.created_by_user_id`), who awarded Stars and Family Chest coins, which Teacher set a lock, and which Admin opened a Child's progress are not in it. Those are staff's ids rather than the Family's own data, so they aren't a finding here. They are recorded so that the fix for #285 decides them too.

**Nothing of another Family's: refuted.** All six fixture Parents, Family A1's and A2's in one class at School A and Family B's at School B, download their Family's file. No file holds another Family's ids, name, Parents' email addresses, Children's nicknames or Character names, devices, Invites, deletion requests or play events. None holds a staff member's id or email address either. Classmates' Rooms are fetched live and appear in no export.

- `exports nothing of another Family's, and nothing of the staff, to any Parent of any Family`

**No secrets: refuted.** No file holds any value stored in `users.password_hash` or `totp_secret`, `children.picture_password_hash`, any `token_hash`, `passkey_challenges.challenge`, `totp_setups.secret` or `recovery_codes.code_hash`. Nor does it hold any fixture credential: password, TOTP secret, recovery code, device token, Invite token or passkey private key. Passkeys' public keys are exported, which is right.

- `leaves every stored password, secret, token and code hash, and every fixture credential, out of every Parent's export`

### Postgres tables: refuted, with the Audit Log documented

When the Admin confirms `parentA1`'s request to delete Ada, no table but `audit_log_entries` holds her id, nickname or Character name, or the id of any event she played. That includes the Gift she bought Ben, which cascades with her. The Audit Log holds her id and neither name, and Ben's rows stay. When the Admin confirms Family B's waiting request, no table but the Audit Log holds any of Family B's ids, name, Parents' email addresses, Child's names, devices, Invites or play. Its Parents' accounts go too. The Audit Log keeps Family B's id, `parentB`'s id and the IP address `parentB` signed in from. This is [ADR 0041](../../architectural-decision-record/0041-security-actions-are-kept-in-an-audit-log.md)'s decision, which records its twelve-month end, and the guide documents the IP address.

- `leaves no row naming Ada outside the Audit Log once the Admin confirms parentA1's request, and only her bare id there`
- `leaves no row naming Family B outside the Audit Log once the Admin confirms its deletion; the Audit Log keeps bare ids, and parentB's sign-in IP address, for its twelve months`

### Container logs: confirmed

`buildHttpServer` creates Fastify with `logger: true`, so every request writes its URL to standard output, and Docker keeps that as the container log. URLs such as `/api/children/:childId/progress` hold Child ids. Many routes log `childId`, `userId` or `familyDeviceId`. There is no `setErrorHandler`, so a query that fails is logged by Fastify's default handler at `error`, with Drizzle's message `Failed query: <SQL>\nparams: <values>` and the error's `params` field. The probe has `parentA1` open Ada's progress, then rename her with a NUL byte in the nickname, which the JSON schema allows and Postgres refuses. The rename answers 500. Its log line holds the SQL, Ada's id, her nickname as sent, and her birth month and year. The Admin then deletes Ada on request, and every one of those lines is still there: nothing the deletion does reaches the log. Neither `docker-compose.yml` nor `docker-compose.pen-test.yml` sets `logging:` for any service. So Docker's default `json-file` driver keeps the log, unbounded unless the daemon is configured otherwise, until the container is removed. ADR 0041 calls the container log "a copy of personal data with no retention (pen-test H14)" and leaves it so. Filed as #282 (medium). _Fixed, unverified (#282)._

- `today_ keeps Ada's id, and the birth month and year a failing query was sent with, in the server's log after Ada is deleted, and no Compose service bounds its log`
- `it.fails`: `keeps no Child's nickname or birth date in the server's log, even from a failing query, and bounds how long Compose keeps the log`

### A Family Device: confirmed

A device keeps what it plays in its outbox until an upload is answered `2xx`. `/api/play/events` refuses a whole batch with 404 when any event in it names a Child who isn't in the device's Family (`ChildNotFoundError`). The device then keeps the batch and sends the same first 100 events every retry. So once Ada is deleted, any of her play still waiting on a device keeps her events there for good. It also blocks every later event behind it, including Ben's, so his play never reaches the server either. The probe uploads one event for Ada and one for Ben from `familyDeviceA1` after Ada's deletion: 404, and neither is stored. A deleted Family's device is answered 401 and drops its playable Family, but its outbox and unsynced play stay in the browser. Filed as #283 (medium). _Fixed, unverified (#283)._ The upload now stores the events naming the device's own Children and lets go of the rest, answering `200` with how many it let go, so the device forgets the batch and a sibling's play gets through. A Child deleted since and one that was never the Family's are answered exactly alike. A device answered 401 clears its outbox and its unsynced play.

- `today_ refuses a Family Device's whole upload once one event in it names a Child deleted since, so a sibling's play in it isn't stored and the device keeps both, and refuses a deleted Family's device outright`
- `it.fails`: `stores the rest of an upload holding play by a Child deleted since, and answers so the device can let the deleted Child's play go`

### Backups on both targets: confirmed as a documented decision

After Family B's deletion, the backup taken before it is still on the NAS stand-in, which `backup.sh` prunes only past `BACKUP_KEEP_NIGHTS` (30 in production). It is still on the bucket stand-in too, which `backup.sh` never prunes. In production, B2's Object Lock keeps each backup for 90 days and no one, the owner included, can remove it sooner ([ADR 0030](../../architectural-decision-record/0030-backups-go-to-the-nas-and-a-locked-b2-bucket.md)). Both periods are decided and recorded, so how long a deleted Family stays in backups is not filed on its own. What a restore does with it is.

- `keeps the backup taken before Family B's deletion on the NAS stand-in, which prunes only past BACKUP_KEEP_NIGHTS, and on the bucket stand-in, which it never prunes`

The stand-ins are directories in the backup container. Object Lock, B2's lifecycle rule and the write-only key aren't reproduced, as [the deployment](../deployment.md#backups) says, and were judged from ADR 0030 and [the backup set-up](../../processes/deploying-to-the-home-server.md#backups).

## A deletion, then a restore: confirmed, the deleted Family comes back

From either stand-in, a restore of the backup taken after the deletion leaves Family B out, which is right. A restore of the backup taken before it brings Family B back whole: Eli, `parentB`'s account with its password hash, and the deletion request, waiting for the Admin again. Nothing in the restored database records that the Family was deleted: the `family.deleted` entry was written after that backup. `restore.sh` restores the dump and nothing else. Nothing in [restoring a backup](../../processes/restoring-a-backup.md) says to apply deletions made since. Sign-ins survive a restore, as that page says, so a deleted Parent's browser is signed in again.

That backup is not an unusual one to restore:

- `restore.sh latest` restores the newest backup, which is up to a day old. Any deletion confirmed since the last night's backup comes back with a restore after a disk failure.
- A restore from a backup chosen by name, such as after a bad migration, brings back every deletion of the nights since.
- A drill restores into `restore-drill`, not the live database, so it brings nothing back, but the drill's database holds the deleted Family until it is torn down.

Filed as #280 (medium). _Fixed, unverified (#280)._

- `leaves Family B out of a restore of the backup taken after its deletion, from either stand-in`
- `today_ brings Family B back from the backup taken before its deletion, from either stand-in: Eli, parentB's account and password, and the deletion request waiting again, with nothing restored to say it was deleted`
- `it.fails`: `keeps Family B out of a restore of any backup once the Admin has confirmed its deletion`

## Who asks for, confirms and carries out a deletion

| Who                                       | Ask (`POST /api/deletion-requests`)  | Confirm (`POST /api/admin/deletion-requests/:id/confirmation`) | Delete with no request (`DELETE /api/admin/children/:id`, `/families/:id`) | Delete in Postgres, in their own reach        |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| A Parent of the Family, either co-Parent  | 201, for a Child or the whole Family | 403                                                            | 403                                                                        | **Yes**, their Children and the Family        |
| A Parent of another Family                | 404 for this Family's Child          | 403                                                            | 403                                                                        | No                                            |
| A Teacher                                 | 403                                  | 403                                                            | 403                                                                        | No (their policies only `SELECT`)             |
| The Admin, signed in with a second factor | 403 (not a Parent)                   | 204                                                            | **204**                                                                    | Yes, by design                                |
| The Admin with a password alone           | 403                                  | 403                                                            | 403                                                                        | —                                             |
| A Family Device                           | 401                                  | 401                                                            | 401                                                                        | **Yes**, its Family's Children and the Family |
| Nobody signed in                          | 401                                  | 401                                                            | 401                                                                        | —                                             |

The probe sends the requests in the first four columns for the Parents, the Teacher, the Admin, the Family Device and nobody, as the tests below list. The cells it doesn't send are read from the route's wrapper (`runAsSignedInParent`, `runAsSignedInAdmin`), and the existing suite already pins a Parent's 403 on the Admin's deletion routes.

**Through the API: refuted.** Only a Parent of the Family asks, and only the Admin, with a second factor, confirms. A confirmation of no request is 404, and Family B stays until the Admin confirms.

- `lets only a Parent of the Family ask for its deletion or a Child's, and only the Admin, signed in with a second factor, confirm one`

Either co-Parent can ask for the whole Family to go, and that deletes the other Parent's account too. The guide documents this ("A Parent chooses a Child, or the whole Family").

**The Admin with no request: confirmed as a documented decision.** The Admin deletes Ben, and Family A2 with both its Parents' accounts, straight from the Admin dashboard. No deletion request exists for either, and no one is emailed, before or after. The guide documents it ("the Admin can delete a Child or a Family directly too"), and the Audit Log records it. But nothing records why the Admin needs it, when it has to stop, or how a Parent learns that their Family has gone. A documented decision with no recorded end is a finding under the PRD. Filed as #284 (low). _Fixed, unverified (#284)._ The Admin no longer deletes what no Parent asked to delete: the two `DELETE` routes refuse with 409 unless a matching deletion request is waiting, and then confirm it as the confirmation route does. The Admin dashboard offers a deletion only beside a Parent's request.

- `today_ lets the Admin delete Ben and Family A2 straight from the Admin dashboard, with no deletion request from their Parents, and tells no one`
- `it.fails`: `deletes a Child or a Family only once one of its Parents has asked for it`

**In Postgres: confirmed.** `families_within_reach`, `children_within_reach` and every other Family policy apply to every command, with the grant holding `DELETE`. So a reach that can read its Family's rows can delete them. Inside a reach transaction, with no API involved, `parentA1`'s reach deletes Ada and `familyDeviceB`'s reach deletes Family B. Each cascades as a confirmed deletion would, except that the Parents' accounts are left. No deletion request is confirmed, and no `child.deleted` or `family.deleted` entry is written. A Teacher's reach deletes nothing. The API alone holds deletion to the Admin's confirmation and the Audit Log. A Family Device, the least trusted credential and one that sits in a Child's hands, holds the most destructive reach in the second wall. Filed as #281 (high: a rule, and the audit with it, enforced in one layer). _Fixed, unverified (#281)._

- `today_ lets parentA1's reach delete Ada, and Family B's device's reach delete Family B, in Postgres, with no deletion request confirmed and no Audit Log entry, while a Teacher's reach deletes nothing`
- `it.fails`: `refuses a Parent's and a Family Device's reach a DELETE on their own Family's Children and Family in Postgres`

## What this round didn't run

The probes were written, typechecked and linted in a sandbox with no container runtime, so the gated run skipped them there. The verdicts above rest on reading the code and on the probes as written, until the gated run is executed on a host with Docker and its output posted on #212. The restore ran against directories standing in for the NAS and the bucket, not against the pen-test deployment's `rclone serve s3` stand-in, and nothing here touched the real NAS or B2. Docker's log retention was judged from the Compose files, since a daemon's own `daemon.json` can set a default the repository can't see. Postgres's own files and the `postgres` container's log were judged by reading, not probed.
