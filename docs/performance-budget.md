# Performance budget

> **Personal use only.** Wizzaroo is intended for your own personal use only. It is not intended for use with or by a school, teacher, parent or student. Only use it for yourself.

The weakest device the Children use is a roughly £150 Android phone from 2021, and it must hold **30 fps**. A 2019 entry-level iPad is the second device to test on. These are the starting limits every scene and asset has to fit. They were set from the slice 1 scene, before anyone measured them on the real devices; the owner confirms or changes them in [#20](https://github.com/adrianeyre/wizzaroo/issues/20) and records the real numbers in the last section.

Read it to me's Piper engine (about 15 MB, 3.7 MB compressed) and its voices (61 to 109 MB each) are not part of the offline download: a device fetches the engine and a voice the first time that voice reads, and keeps them in the `wizzaroo-voices` cache ([0062](./architectural-decision-record/0062-read-it-to-me-speaks-with-every-piper-voice-on-the-device.md)).

Sound effects and each Region's looping tune are made in the browser from a few kilobytes of notes in the Island themes, so they add no audio files to the offline download; `src/client/game-sound.test.ts` plays every one of them and checks nothing is fetched and the notes stay under 16 KB.

A Game played from a Region on Islands 2 to 6 dresses its stage with two of that Island's props, within 100 triangles and one mesh (`src/client/islands/island-stage-props.tsx`). So that every dressed scene still fits, each stage family's test checks its scenes against 9,900 triangles and 39 meshes. Music's stages are not dressed: their busiest scene draws 9,972 triangles.

The pixel style is what makes these limits generous for listen-and-pick: the world is drawn at a quarter of the screen's resolution or less, then scaled up, so the GPU fills few pixels however many there are on the screen. Popping bubbles is drawn at the screen's own resolution, like the World Map below ([0013](./architectural-decision-record/0013-popping-bubbles-is-drawn-smooth.md)), but its bubbles are two triangles each, so it stays far inside the limits.

The World Map is the other exception ([0012](./architectural-decision-record/0012-the-world-map-is-drawn-in-full-detail.md)). It is drawn at the screen's own resolution, up to twice the CSS pixels on a high-density screen, so it is allowed many more triangles but fills every pixel. Its trees, bushes, rocks and houses are instanced, and every landmark is one merged mesh, which keeps its draw calls low. The sea's depth is one 256 × 176 texture, well under the texture limits. Rain, snow and blowing leaves are lines and points moved by the GPU, a few thousand at most, so weather adds almost nothing to the CPU's work.

## Automatic resolution and effects scaling

The game measures its own frame rate once a second (`src/client/render-quality.ts`):

1. **Below 30 fps**, it lowers its render resolution one step: each pixel of the world grows from 4 screen pixels to 5, then 6, then 8.
2. **Still below 30 fps at 8**, it drops the effects, such as the sparkles when a bubble pops.

On the World Map and in popping bubbles the same steps lower the resolution instead of growing pixels: the full resolution, then four-fifths, two-thirds and a half of it. Dropping the effects there turns off the map's shadows and clouds, and the burst when a bubble pops. 3. **At 50 fps or more for 5 seconds in a row**, it raises the quality back one step, so a moment of headroom never makes it flicker between two levels.

A gap of more than half a second between frames, as when the game was hidden and comes back, never counts as slow.

## The performance overlay

Open the game page with `?performance` on the end of its address, such as `https://wizzaroo.adrianeyre.co.uk/?performance`, and every scene shows a line along its bottom with the frame rate, the resolution the world is drawn at, its pixel size, the draw calls in the last frame, and whether effects are on. It is the same build the Children play; only the address differs.
