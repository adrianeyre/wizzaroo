# Email

[← Back to the README](../../README.md) · [All guides](README.md)

> **Personal use only.** Wizzaroo is intended for your own personal use only. It is not intended for use with or by a school, teacher, parent or student. Only use it for yourself.

Wizzaroo sends email only to Teachers and Parents, through the self-hosted [email platform](https://github.com/adrianeyre/email-platform), a service of its own that renders each email from a template and hands it to an SMTP provider. Wizzaroo doesn't render or send email itself, and holds no SMTP settings ([ADR 0069](../architectural-decision-record/0069-emails-are-sent-through-the-email-platform.md)).

- **Wizzaroo chooses the template and fills its variables.** Each email is one of the email platform's 15 built-in Wizzaroo templates, sent by its slug, such as `wizzaroo-reset-your-password`, with the `@adrianeyre/email-client` package. `src/server/email/emails.ts` builds each one: which template, to whom, and its variables. Dates and times are written out in UK time, such as "14 November 2026 at 13:52 (UK time)", lifetimes as words, such as "24 hours" or "7 days", and a new email address is masked, such as `m•••@pen-test.example`. [The emails](emails.md) lists every template and its variables.
- **The email platform holds everything else.** The subject lines, the wording, the HTML layout and its plain-text part, the Wizzaroo header and footer, and the from address, `Wizzaroo <no-reply@wizzaroo.adrianeyre.co.uk>`, are in the email platform's templates and their Wizzaroo configuration. Changing what an email says is a change there, not here. It escapes every variable in the HTML, and security notices hold no link.
- **Sending.** `src/server/email/send-email.ts` makes one client for the email platform at `EMAIL_BASE_URL`, signed in with `EMAIL_API_KEY`, and calls the template's helper on `email.builtIn`, such as `resetYourPassword`. A send is done once the email platform has accepted and queued the email; it then delivers it through its own SMTP provider, retrying if that fails, and shows it in its **Logs**. A refusal, a network failure or no answer within 10 seconds fails the send.

No email ever holds a Child's details: each one is built from Parents' email addresses and the link or time it needs, and nothing else. An Invite also names the adult and their School, and a Family join request notice the School: every name an email carries is reduced to one plain line as the email is built, with format and bidirectional controls dropped and each run of line breaks and spaces made one space, so even a name stored before such names were refused can't make a paragraph of its own (#321). Names are refused with a line break, a control or a link in them when they are typed, and an address is refused unless Nodemailer's address parser reads it as exactly that one mailbox, so the address an account stores, checks for uniqueness and confirms is the one its email goes to (#322, [signing in](signing-in.md#schools-teachers-and-invites)). Where the email platform is, and the key it gave Wizzaroo, are configuration, not code:

| Variable         | What it is                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `PUBLIC_URL`     | Where Parents reach Wizzaroo, which links in emails start with                                                                                                                       |
| `EMAIL_BASE_URL` | Where the email platform runs, as an `http` or `https` URL. In development it is `http://localhost:4320`, the email platform's own development port                                  |
| `EMAIL_API_KEY`  | An API key made in the email platform, under **API keys** or with `pnpm cli apikey:create`, starting `ek_`. It is a secret: at least 8 characters, not counting spaces at either end |

The server refuses an `EMAIL_API_KEY` under 8 characters, and `.env.example`'s `ek_development_key_from_the_local_email_platform` outside development, as it does every example secret ([development](development.md), [ADR 0054](../architectural-decision-record/0054-configuration-refuses-weak-and-development-secrets.md)).

The Parent dashboard's **Your account** panel shows a Parent's email address and whether it is confirmed.

- **Confirming an email address.** A Parent asks for a link to confirm their address, or a new one. Wizzaroo emails a link to that address which works once and stops working after 24 hours; asking again replaces it. The database holds only a hash of it, and the secret is in the link's fragment (`/parent/#confirm-email=…`), which browsers never send to the server, so it can't reach a log. Opening the link and tapping **Confirm my email address** confirms it, signed in or not. A new address takes over from the old one only once it is confirmed; until then the Parent still signs in with the old one. The owner's address from `pnpm seed:owner` starts unconfirmed.
- **Changing an email address takes a fresh proof.** The address decides where every reset link and notice goes, so it is a credential: asking for a link to a new address needs, in the same request, the current password, a code from the authenticator app (or a recovery code), or the adult's own passkey, and for Teachers and the Admin always the code or the passkey, never the password alone. A code that has already been used, the one that signed them in included, is refused. A Teacher's or the Admin's sign-in that hasn't used a second factor can't ask at all. Without the proof Wizzaroo answers `403` and sends nothing, so a stolen sign-in cookie alone can't move the account's address and then reset its password. A link to the address the account already has moves nothing, so it needs no proof.
- **Telling the old address.** When a move to a new address is asked for, and again when it is confirmed, Wizzaroo emails the address the account has, if it is confirmed: what is changing, to which new address, shown masked as its first letter and its domain (`m•••@pen-test.example`), when, and what to do if it wasn't them. Neither email holds a link, so neither can change anything. The first is sent before the check is saved and the second before the change is saved, so if either can't be sent nothing changes. Every notice after the move goes to the new address, so the old one hears of the move itself.
- **An address that is taken.** Asking to move to an address another account already has, staff ones included, answers `204` with no body, exactly as a free address does, after the same proof. Wizzaroo sends that address a short note saying someone tried to use it and nothing changed, with no link, and sends no confirmation link anywhere; any check the adult had waiting is cancelled, as asking again always does. Only whoever reads that mailbox learns it is taken. Opening a link to an address that was taken since it was sent is still refused (`409`).
- **Confirming a move.** Confirming a new address cancels every reset link and other address check the account had, and ends the adult's sign-ins but for the confirming browser's own ([signing in](signing-in.md#passkeys-and-two-factor-authentication)).
- **Only a confirmed address counts.** `users.email_confirmed_at` is set only by opening a link, and an address waiting to be confirmed lives in `email_address_checks`, never in `users`. Anything that emails a Parent about their account, such as a password reset, must use an address whose `email_confirmed_at` is set.
- **Changing a password.** A signed-in Parent changes their password by giving the current one and a new one of at least 12 characters. Their other browsers are signed out, any reset link or address check they had waiting is cancelled, and if their address is confirmed Wizzaroo emails it to say the password changed. That email is sent before the change is saved, so if it can't be sent the password stays as it was. Setting a password where there was none, and removing it, cancel them too.
- **Turning two-factor authentication off.** When an adult turns it off ([signing in](signing-in.md#passkeys-and-two-factor-authentication)), Wizzaroo emails their confirmed address to say when, that their recovery codes no longer work, and what to do if it wasn't them, since a signed-in session alone can turn it off. As with a password change, the email is sent before the change is saved, so if it can't be sent two-factor authentication stays on. A wrong code sends nothing.
- **Passkeys.** Adding a passkey, in the browser or from a QR code on a phone, and removing one each email the adult's confirmed address a security notice: what changed, the passkey's name, when, and what to do if it wasn't them, with no link. Like the password-changed notice, it is sent before the change is saved, so if it can't be sent the passkey isn't added or removed. An unconfirmed address is sent nothing.
- **Wrong sign-in codes.** When 5 wrong two-factor codes in an hour pause an adult's codes ([signing in](signing-in.md#passkeys-and-two-factor-authentication)), Wizzaroo emails their confirmed address to say so, with when the pause ends, and that if it wasn't them someone knows their password. It is sent once the pause is saved; if it can't be sent, that is logged and the code is still refused.
- **Resetting a forgotten password.** **Forgot your password?** on the sign-in form asks for an email address, and always answers the same way: if it's the confirmed address of an account, a link is on its way. Wizzaroo answers without waiting to look the address up, so neither the answer nor how long it takes says which addresses have accounts, and an address that isn't confirmed gets nothing. The link (`/parent/#reset-password=…`) works once and stops working after 1 hour; asking again replaces it, and `password_resets` holds only a hash of it. Each address can be asked for only so often, and each caller can ask only so often, by [the request-rate limits](security-and-backups.md#request-rate-limits); past either, the answer is `429`, the same for both and for an address with no account. Each link emailed is kept in the Audit Log, as is the first refusal for an adult's address, so an inbox someone is trying to flood is on record. Choosing a new password of at least 12 characters from it signs out every browser signed in to the account, cancels any address check waiting to be confirmed, and sends the password-changed notice, which, as with changing a password, is sent before the change is saved. The link is checked before anything else, so a wrong or used one is refused (`404`) before the new password is hashed. A Parent with two-factor authentication on still needs a code to sign in afterwards.

### When an email is sent

No email is sent while the server holds a database connection, so a slow email platform delays emails and nothing else: sign-ins, dashboards, Family Devices and `/health` keep answering (#329).

- **Links and Invites** (a reset link, an address check, an Invite) are saved first and sent once that has committed. A link that can't be sent is taken back, so none is left waiting that nobody received; an Invite that can't be sent is kept and shown as not sent, as a bulk upload's is, and the request answers with an error so the sender knows to send a new one. An Invite accepted from a copied link still makes the login if its address check can't be sent; the adult asks for another check once signed in.
- **Security notices** (a password changed or reset, two-factor turned off, a passkey added or removed, a Family Device signed in, an email address moving) are still sent before the change is saved, so if one can't be sent nothing changes. Sent here means the email platform has accepted it: one it accepts and later fails to deliver, after its own retries, shows as `failed` in its **Logs**, and the change still stands. The server checks the change can be made, sends the notice holding no connection, then saves the change, checking again. A change that fails that second check, because something changed in between, leaves a notice for a change that didn't happen.
- **Other notices** (an adult joining a Family, a Teacher asking for one to, wrong sign-in codes) are sent once their change is saved.
- **Timeouts.** The server gives the email platform 10 seconds to answer each send before it fails (`emailPlatformTimeoutMs` in `src/server/email/send-email.ts`, the client's `timeoutMs`). And a query waits at most 10 seconds for one of the database pool's connections before it fails, rather than for ever.

### Running it locally

Wizzaroo's development stack has no mail catcher of its own. Run the email platform from [its own repo](https://github.com/adrianeyre/email-platform), as [its local setup](https://github.com/adrianeyre/email-platform/blob/main/docs/getting-started/local-setup.md) says. Its development stack has its own Mailpit, on 1025 (SMTP) and <http://localhost:8025> (its inbox), and its UI and API are at <http://localhost:4320>. Then, in the email platform:

1. Under **Delivery**, point the SMTP provider at its Mailpit: host `localhost`, port `1025`, no TLS and no username. Nothing is then delivered to a real inbox.
2. Add the built-in templates, with their Wizzaroo header, footer and configuration:

   ```bash
   pnpm cli templates:add-built-ins
   ```

3. Make an API key, under **API keys** or with `pnpm cli apikey:create "Wizzaroo development"`. It is shown once.

Put that key in Wizzaroo's `.env` as `EMAIL_API_KEY`, in place of `.env.example`'s, and leave `EMAIL_BASE_URL` as `http://localhost:4320`. Every email Wizzaroo sends then shows in the email platform's **Logs**, and lands in its Mailpit at <http://localhost:8025>. Without the email platform running, every send fails, and so does whatever needs it, such as asking for a reset link or changing a password.
