# Parent limits and game sound

[← Back to the README](../../README.md) · [All guides](README.md)

> **Personal use only.** Wizzaroo is intended for your own personal use only. It is not intended for use with or by a school, teacher, parent or student. Only use it for yourself.

## Parent limits

On the **Children** panel, **Set (name)'s limits** sets a Child's daily time, in minutes, and which Subjects they can play. They are held in `children.synced_limits`, sent to each Family Device with the Children, and kept by the device from what it last synced, so a change applies after the device next goes online. Time is counted per device, every second the game is showing, and kept in `localStorage`, so two devices together may go over a daily limit. The server keeps no daily limit of its own and counts all the time a device reports; [ADR 0004](../architectural-decision-record/0004-offline-play-records-events-the-server-decides.md#when-this-decision-ends) accepts that only until a second School is added. When a Child reaches the limit, the device ends the Session and says that's all for today, with no network needed, and the Child can't start again on that device until tomorrow. Missions in a Subject that isn't allowed are left off the World Map.

The same form has **Play music** and **Play sound effects**, both on until a Parent turns them off, held in the synced limits as `isMusicMuted` and `areSoundEffectsMuted`. Each Family Device follows them after it next syncs. Neither ever silences voice-over, so a Child always hears the questions, or the Music Region's own clips, which are what its Games teach.

**Let (name) explore the Islands on foot** is on until a Parent turns it off, held in the synced limits as `isExploringLocked`. Locked, the Family Device hides every way in to exploring: the Explore tab in the play bar (on the World Map, Wardrobe, Room and Settings), the **Explore (Island)** button and the drag-me walker on the World Map, and Explore in the Menu's View panel. The Child still plays every Game from the World Map. A limits change that doesn't mention the lock, as from a dashboard that predates it, keeps the one stored.

Under it, **Islands (name) can explore** has a switch for each Island, every one on until a Parent turns it off, held in the synced limits as `exploreLockedIslandIds` ([ADR 0065](../architectural-decision-record/0065-parents-lock-single-islands-for-exploring.md)). A locked Island isn't offered anywhere the Child could set off to explore it: there is no **Explore (Island)** button or Menu Explore while it is in view on the World Map, the walker can't be dropped on it, its Year is left off the Islands to travel to while exploring, and the Explore tab starts on the Child's home Island, or the first open one if that is locked. With every Island locked, Explore is hidden as it is with exploring locked. The Child still plays the locked Island's Games from the World Map. A limits change that doesn't mention the Islands keeps the ones stored.

### Islands to play

The Parent page's **Islands** page has a card for each Island, with a **Regions open** switch ([ADR 0071](../architectural-decision-record/0071-islands-above-a-childs-year-are-explored-but-not-played-by-default.md)). By default it is on for the Islands for the Child's own Year and below, and off for those above, badged "Locked: above (name)'s Year". Turning it on for an Island above opens it early, held in the synced limits as `unlockedIslandIds` and badged "Opened early"; turning it off for an Island at or below their Year locks it, held as `lockedIslandIds` and badged "Locked by you". Turning either back returns the Island to its default. A Parent's lock wins over an early opening, and a Teacher's lock wins over both ([ADR 0035](../architectural-decision-record/0035-teachers-lock-what-a-child-plays.md)). The switch decides only what the Child plays: they can travel to and explore every Island whatever it says, unless it is locked for exploring above. Each Region and Game on the card has its own switch, and **All Games open** opens every Game in a Region at once; these are kept while the Island is locked and apply once it is open.

## Game sound

Besides voice-over, the game makes chiptune sound in the browser with the Web Audio API (`src/client/game-sound.ts`), so it adds no files to the offline download:

- **Sound effects.** A blip for every tap on a button while playing, a rising arpeggio for a right answer, two soft low notes for a wrong one, a sparkle when a Mission earns Stars, and a fanfare when it unlocks a Game or an Item. Tapping the Stars in the play bar sets off fireworks with their own sounds, in step with what is drawn: a whistle as each rocket rises, a boom and crackles as it bursts into stars, and a twinkle for each shooting star (`playFireworkSounds` in `src/client/game-sound.ts`, cued from the same show by `listFireworkSoundCues` in `src/client/mission/star-fireworks.tsx`). Like every sound effect they are made in the browser, from white noise and swept tones, and are silent with sound effects off. Opening the Family Chest and the Awards each plays a little piece of its own, layered like a film's sound effect and run through one compressor so it hits hard without clipping (`src/client/opening-sounds.ts`). **Treasure!**, for the Family Chest, is in C major: the lid thunks and its hinge creaks, a harp sweeps up two octaves on a rush of air, brass blasts "ta… DAAA!" over a timpani boom, a sub bass and a cymbal shimmer, and gold coins clink and tumble out. **WOW!**, for the Awards, is in D major: a slide whistle zooms up into a three-voice choir singing a swooping "wowww", shaped by two formant filters as a mouth would, then glockenspiel sparkles climb to a double "ding!" while a crowd claps.
- **A looping tune for each Region.** Each Island's theme has its music (`theme.music` in `src/curriculum/world-map.json` and `src/curriculum/islands.json`): a tempo and a short tune for each of its Regions, written as up to 32 notes or rests, such as `"C5 E5 - G5"`, each half a beat long. The tune loops quietly while a Child plays a Game in that Region and stops when they leave it. A loop is skipped while the Music Region plays its own clips.
- **Silent mode and reduced motion.** The game asks the browser for ambient audio where it offers that (`navigator.audioSession`, in Safari), so the device's silent switch silences the music and effects. A device set to reduce motion gets no looping music, only the effects.
